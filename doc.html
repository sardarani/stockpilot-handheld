Back to the case study

Count blind. Confirm once. Keep walking.

Meena needs to resolve a 24-unit gap at bin 14-C-03 without stopping her shift. The design adds accuracy where it’s cheap, protects speed where it’s expensive, and never makes her wait for someone else’s decision.

How the physical environment shaped the design

  • Gloves and a box in one hand. 56px pill buttons, 52px keys, whole-row targets and yellow side buttons to scan, with no typing anywhere. Primary actions sit at the bottom, in thumb reach. The one action that commits a change, sending for approval, is hold-to-send, so a bump can’t trigger it.
  • Noise. Feedback is felt and seen, not heard: one vibration for a match, three for a gap, and the scanner’s LED glows green, amber or red.
  • Varying light. Near-black on white (about 20:1 contrast), 56–64px numerals and bold sentence-case type. Blue marks the one action to press, red appears only for a gap that needs attention, and green only for a confirmed match. Status is never shown by colour alone. In dim aisles, the light sensor flips the screen to a dark theme.
  • Standing and moving. Each screen answers one question and has one blue primary button. The bin code and the gap are the largest things on screen.

How I balanced speed with accuracy

  • Accuracy where it’s cheap. A blind count, so the system’s 120 can’t anchor her. Counting in cartons of 12. One recommended (not forced) recount when the gap is over 10%. Check digits instead of typed bin codes when a label won’t scan. Reasons chosen from a list, with the matching evidence shown.
  • Speed where it’s expensive. Matches and gaps within 10% save straight away. Approval runs in the background: she sends it, walks to the next bin and gets a buzz when Ravi decides. If Ravi is beside her, his PIN settles it there. Undo replaces confirmation dialogs.

Assumptions

  • Units are pairs, 12 per carton. The 10% rule compares against the system quantity in either direction; within 10%, the count saves without approval.
  • Each bin holds one form of stock, either full cartons or loose units. A bin with both is out of scope; the counter enters the total in units.
  • Supervisors can approve from their own device, and the warehouse system knows about unconfirmed picks for each bin.
  • The device is a rugged Android scanner (360 × 740) with yellow scan triggers; system sheets and snackbars follow Material 3.

Design language: Uber’s, via getdesign.md, adapted with a blue primary, a red attention colour and glove-sized targets. Patterns adapted from uiarc, spectrum ui, kobra, spaceui, useplanes, bencho, easyui, lightswind and others; rebuilt in plain code for a low-end device.
